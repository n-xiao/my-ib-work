Mass defect $\implies$ the mass of a nucleus $<$ total mass of its constituent parts
$\implies$ nuclear binding energy
$\because$ energy and mass are considered interchangeable:
	$E = mc^2$
	where $c$ is the speed of light

Binding energy originates inside the nucleus. **Protons are positively charged** and repel each other through [[Coulomb's law]]. 
Hence, there must be an additional attractive interaction that opposes this repulsion to **hold the nucleus together**.

An attractive **strong nuclear interaction** (strong nuclear force) acts between the protons and neutrons.

At **short distances**,
	strong nuclear force is **repulsive**;
	protons and neutrons are repelled
At **long distances**,
	strong nuclear force is **attractive**;
	protons and neutrons are attracted to each other;
	it binds the protons and neutrons together

At distance of 1 fm = $1\times 10^{-15}$ m
	$\text{strong attraction} \approx 100 \times \text{EM repulsion}$

Energy must be added to a nucleus to separate it into its component parts.
Hence, to form a nucleus by bringing its individual nucleons together from infinity, energy must be **transferred out of the nucleus**. This links to [[electric potential]].

This is the source of **binding energy** = **mass defect**.
