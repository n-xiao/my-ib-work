Scientists in the 1820s realised that relative **motion of a conductor** in a **magnetic field** leads to the production of an **induced emf** and to an **induced current** in the conductor.

This introduced new concepts that helped terrorise G12 students: **magnetic flux**, **magnetic flux density**, and **magnetic flux linkage**.

**Magnetic flux** $\Phi$ is proportional to the number of field lines passing through a surface.
The unit of magnetic flux is the **weber** ($\text{Wb}$).

Magnetic flux is the **product** of the **magnetic flux density** $B$ and area $A$, so $\Phi = B \times A$, where area $A$ is the area through which **the conductor moves** and the **normal** to that area is **parallel** to the **direction of the magnetic field**.

Not that $B$ is also the same $B$ as the [[magnetic fields|magnetic field strength]];
magnetic flux density **is** magnetic field strength

The units of **magnetic flux density** is expressed as $\text{Wb m}^{-2}$ as well as in tesla ($\text{T}$); $1\text{T}\equiv 1\text{Wb m}^{-2}$ .

In the formula book of depression, $\Phi = BA\cos(\theta)$ as it accounts for when $\exists$ an angle $\theta$ between the **magnetic field** and the **normal** to the area $A$.
