Newton's law of gravitation $\implies$ $F \propto \frac{m_1\times m_2}{r^2}$ : $r$ is the distance between two point masses and where $F$ is an attractive force ($F<0\text{ N}$).

For an object on Earth's surface, the gravitational force is:
$$
\begin{aligned}
F&=\frac{Gm\times m_E}{{r_E}^2}\\
\text{Weight} &= mg\\
mg&=\frac{Gm\times m_E}{{r_E}^2}\\
\therefore g&=\frac{G\times m_E}{{r_E}^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

$g$ is the gravitational field strength. 
Not exclusive to Earth and can be used anywhere in a gravitational field,
$$
\begin{aligned}
	g&=\frac{\text{force acting on a small test object}}{\text{mass of the test object}}
	=\frac{F}{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

$g$ has two units:
	$\text{N kg}^{-1}$ when dealing with gravitational field strength
	$\text{ms}^{-1}$ when dealing with acceleration of free fall

**Test objects** are required because objects can contribute to the gravitational field where it is placed. Test objects would have negligible effect on the original gravitational field it is placed in since $\text{mass of test object} \ll \text{mass of object producing the field}$.
