**Electric potential** is a property of **an electric field**, not of individual objects within the field. It is a quantity that is **independent** of the magnitude of the charge on the object on which the force acts.

**Electric potential** is a **scalar** quantity.

**Electric potential** $V_{\text{e}}$ in a radial field is given by $V_{\text{e}}=\frac{kq}{r}$ where $q$ is the charge that **produces** the radial field. This follows from [[Coulomb's law]] and the relationship between field strength and potential.

**Electric potential energy** is the energy (work) required to bring two point charges $q_1$ and $q_2$ **from infinity** to a separation of $r$; $E_{\text{p}} = k\frac{q_1\times q_2}{r}$.

There is zero potential at infinity.

The work done $W$ in moving a charge $q$ in an electric field is $W=q\times \Delta V_{\text{e}}$, where $\Delta V_{\text{e}}$ is the **electric potential difference**.

The unit of electric potential is $\text{J C}^{-1} \equiv V$. 

When there are many separate charges, each one has to be brought from $\infty$, so the total electric potential energy is the **sum of each separate energy transfer**.

$$
\text{electric field strength E} = -\frac{\Delta V_\text{e}}{\Delta r}
$$
